# Bank Accounts and Deposits

General educational information about the main types of bank accounts and deposit products.
Terms, fees, and rules vary by country and bank; always check your bank's specific product
documents.

## Why bank accounts exist

A bank account keeps money safe, lets you receive income and pay bills electronically,
builds a financial record useful for loans and visas, and (for deposits) earns interest. In
most countries, bank deposits are protected up to a limit by a government-backed insurance
scheme.

**Deposit insurance:** In India, the DICGC insures deposits up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor
per bank. In the United States, the FDIC insures up to $250,000 per depositor per insured
bank per ownership category. Amounts above the limit are not protected if the bank fails.

## Savings account

- Designed for individuals to hold money not needed for daily spending.
- Pays modest interest (often calculated on the daily balance and paid quarterly).
- May limit the number of free withdrawals per month.
- Often requires a **minimum balance** (monthly average balance); falling below it triggers
  a fee. "Zero-balance" / Basic Savings Bank Deposit Accounts (BSBDA) in India have no such
  requirement but limit some services.
- Good for: emergency funds, short-term savings, receiving salary (a dedicated "salary
  account" is a savings account with the minimum-balance rule waived while the employer pays
  in).

## Current account (checking account)

- Designed for businesses and people with a high volume of transactions.
- Usually pays **no interest**.
- No cap on the number of transactions.
- Often comes with an **overdraft** facility (permission to go below zero up to a limit,
  with interest charged on the negative amount).
- Higher minimum-balance requirements than savings accounts.

## Fixed deposit (FD) / term deposit / certificate of deposit (CD)

- You lock a lump sum for a chosen period (7 days to 10 years in India) at a fixed interest
  rate agreed upfront.
- Interest is typically higher than a savings account and does not change even if market
  rates fall during the term.
- **Premature withdrawal** is usually allowed but with a penalty (e.g. 0.5–1% lower
  interest).
- Interest can be paid periodically (payout FD) or added back and compounded
  (cumulative/reinvestment FD).
- Senior citizens often get a small extra interest rate.
- **Tax-saving FD:** a 5-year FD that qualifies for a tax deduction under Section 80C in
  India, but it cannot be broken early.

## Recurring deposit (RD)

- You commit to depositing a fixed amount every month for a set term (e.g. 12–60 months) at
  a fixed rate.
- Builds a savings habit; the maturity value is the sum of instalments plus compounded
  interest.
- Missing instalments usually attracts a small penalty.

## Sweep-in / auto-sweep / flexi deposit

- Links a savings or current account to an FD. Balance above a threshold is automatically
  moved into an FD to earn more; if the main account runs short, money is pulled back
  ("reverse sweep") in small units. Aims to combine liquidity with higher returns.

## Joint accounts and operating instructions

- A joint account is held by two or more people. Operating mode can be:
  - **Either or survivor** — any one holder can operate; on death of one, the survivor
    continues.
  - **Jointly** — all holders must sign for every transaction.
  - **Former or survivor** — only the first holder operates while alive.
- A **nominee** should be registered so funds pass smoothly if a holder dies. A nominee
  receives the money as a trustee; legal heirs may still have a claim.

## Common fees to watch for

- Below-minimum-balance charge
- ATM withdrawal charges beyond the free monthly limit (especially at other banks' ATMs)
- Debit card annual fee
- Cheque book charges beyond a free quota
- SMS alert charges
- Failed auto-debit / bounced cheque / return charges
- Account closure fee if closed within a short window (e.g. 14 days to 12 months) of opening
- Currency conversion / foreign transaction markup on international card use

## Opening an account: what you typically need (KYC)

- **Proof of identity:** passport, driving licence, voter ID, Aadhaar, PAN (India);
  government photo ID / SSN (US).
- **Proof of address:** utility bill, rental agreement, passport, Aadhaar.
- A recent photograph and, in India, a PAN or Form 60.
- Non-residents use specific account types (e.g. NRE / NRO / FCNR accounts in India).

## Dormant / inactive accounts

If there are no customer-initiated transactions for a long period (two years in India), an
account becomes **inactive** and then **dormant**, and some services are frozen until you
re-verify your KYC. Unclaimed balances after ten years are transferred to a central fund
(the DEA Fund in India) but can still be reclaimed.

## Choosing between products (educational framing, not advice)

- Money you may need any day → savings account or a liquid form.
- Money you will not touch for months and want a guaranteed return → FD.
- Building savings from monthly income → RD or an automated transfer to savings.
- Business cash flow with many payments → current account.
Diversifying where you keep cash (and staying within deposit-insurance limits per bank) is a
common risk-management practice.
