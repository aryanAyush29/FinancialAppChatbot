# Credit Cards and Credit Scores

Educational information on how credit cards work and how credit scores are built and used.

## How a credit card works

1. The issuer gives you a **credit limit** — the maximum you can owe at once.
2. When you pay a merchant, the issuer pays them and adds the amount to your balance.
3. Once per **billing cycle** (about a month) you get a **statement** showing the total
   balance, the **minimum amount due**, and the **payment due date**.
4. If you pay the **full statement balance by the due date**, purchases are interest-free —
   this is the **grace period** (typically ~20–50 days from purchase, depending on timing).
5. If you pay less than the full amount, the unpaid balance "revolves" and is charged
   interest, usually from the transaction date, with **no** grace period until you clear it
   fully again.

### Why paying only the minimum is expensive

The minimum due is often just 5% of the balance. Card interest is quoted monthly (e.g.
3–3.75% per month) which is roughly 36–45% per year. Paying only the minimum can stretch a
modest balance over many years and cost more in interest than the original purchases.

## Common card charges

- **Annual / joining fee** (sometimes waived above a yearly spend threshold)
- **Finance charge / interest** on revolved balances and on all new purchases while a
  balance is revolving
- **Cash advance fee** — withdrawing cash on a credit card typically costs a flat fee plus
  interest from day one, with no grace period
- **Late payment fee** — a slab based on the outstanding amount
- **Over-limit fee**
- **Foreign currency markup** — often 1.5–3.5% on international transactions
- **GST / tax** on fees and charges
- **EMI conversion fee** and interest when you convert a large purchase to instalments

## Card safety features and habits

- **Tokenisation** for online and in-app payments so merchants never store the real number.
- **OTP / 3-D Secure** for online transactions.
- Set per-channel limits (online, international, contactless) and switch off what you do not
  use.
- Check statements for unfamiliar charges; report disputes quickly (chargeback rights are
  time-limited).
- Never share the CVV, PIN, or OTP; block the card immediately if lost.

## Credit reports and credit scores

A **credit report** is a detailed history of your borrowing: every loan and card, credit
limits, balances, payment history, enquiries, and any defaults or settlements. In India it
is maintained by credit bureaus — **CIBIL (TransUnion), Experian, Equifax, CRIF High Mark**.
In the U.S. the main bureaus are Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.

A **credit score** is a three-digit summary of that report. In India the common range is
**300–900**; roughly 750+ is generally viewed as strong. U.S. FICO and VantageScore run
300–850.

### What typically influences a score

| Factor | Rough weight | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Payment history | Highest | Paying every EMI and card bill on time, in full |
| Credit utilisation | High | Keeping card balances well below the limit (often <30% suggested) |
| Age / length of history | Medium | Keeping old accounts open and active |
| Credit mix | Lower | A healthy blend of secured and unsecured credit, handled well |
| New credit / enquiries | Lower | Not applying for many loans or cards in a short span |

### How scores are used

Lenders, and sometimes landlords, insurers, and employers (where permitted), use the score
and report to decide whether to approve credit and at what interest rate. A higher score
generally means easier approval and a lower rate.

### Checking and correcting your report

- You are entitled to your credit report; each bureau in India must provide one free full
  report per calendar year, and many banks/apps show the score for free.
- Checking your **own** score is a "soft enquiry" and does **not** lower it. Only a lender's
  "hard enquiry" when you apply can nudge it down slightly.
- If you find an error (a loan that isn't yours, a wrongly reported default), file a dispute
  with the bureau; they must investigate, typically within about 30 days.

### Building or rebuilding a score (educational, not advice)

- Pay on time, every time — set auto-pay for at least the minimum as a safety net, but aim
  to pay in full.
- Keep utilisation low; ask for a limit increase or spread spending, rather than maxing one
  card.
- Avoid closing your oldest card without reason.
- Space out credit applications.
- If you have no history, a **secured credit card** (backed by an FD) or being added as an
  authorised user can establish one.
- A "settled" or "written off" status hurts more than "closed"; try to pay in full rather
  than settle.

## Debit card vs. credit card (quick contrast)

| | Debit card | Credit card |
|---|---|---|
| Source of funds | Your bank balance | Issuer's credit line |
| Can you overspend? | No (beyond balance/overdraft) | Yes, up to the limit |
| Builds credit history? | No | Yes |
| Interest | None | High, if balance revolves |
| Fraud liability | Can be higher; funds leave immediately | Often stronger protection; you dispute before paying |
