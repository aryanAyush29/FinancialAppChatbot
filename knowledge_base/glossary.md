# Financial & Banking Glossary

A plain-language glossary of common banking and personal-finance terms. Definitions are
general and educational, not advice.

## A

**Account statement** — A periodic summary (usually monthly) of all transactions on an
account, showing opening balance, deposits, withdrawals, charges, interest, and closing
balance.

**Accrued interest** — Interest that has been earned or has built up over time but has not
yet been paid out or added to the balance.

**Amortisation** — The process of paying off a loan through regular equal instalments, where
each instalment covers both interest and part of the principal. Early instalments are mostly
interest; later ones are mostly principal.

**Annual Percentage Rate (APR)** — The yearly cost of borrowing expressed as a percentage,
including interest and certain fees. It lets borrowers compare loan offers on a common basis.

**Annuity** — A financial product that pays out a fixed stream of income at regular
intervals, often used for retirement.

**Appreciation** — An increase in the value of an asset over time (opposite of
depreciation).

**Asset** — Anything of value that a person or business owns, such as cash, bank deposits,
property, or investments.

**ATM (Automated Teller Machine)** — A machine that lets customers withdraw cash, check
balances, and perform basic banking tasks using a debit or credit card and a PIN.

**Auto-debit / standing instruction** — An arrangement authorising a bank to automatically
pay a recurring bill or transfer a fixed amount on set dates.

## B

**Balance** — The amount of money currently in an account. "Available balance" may be lower
than "ledger balance" if some funds are on hold or cheques are uncleared.

**Bank rate / policy rate** — The interest rate at which a country's central bank lends to
commercial banks. It influences the interest rates banks charge customers.

**Bankruptcy / insolvency** — A legal status for a person or company that cannot repay its
debts. A court process then decides how creditors are paid.

**Base rate / MCLR / RLLR** — Reference rates banks use to set loan interest. In India,
newer floating-rate retail loans are usually linked to an external benchmark (RLLR, often
tied to the RBI repo rate); older loans used MCLR or the base rate.

**Beneficiary** — The person or account that receives money in a transfer, or the person
entitled to receive proceeds from an insurance policy or account on the holder's death.

**Bond** — A debt security. The buyer lends money to a government or company and receives
periodic interest (the "coupon") plus the face value back at maturity.

**Bounced cheque / dishonoured cheque** — A cheque the bank refuses to pay, usually due to
insufficient funds or a signature mismatch. It often carries penalties.

**Budget** — A plan that compares expected income with expected expenses over a period so
spending and saving can be managed deliberately.

## C

**Capital gain** — The profit made when an asset is sold for more than its purchase price.
Many countries tax this.

**Cheque (check)** — A written, signed order instructing a bank to pay a stated sum from the
writer's account to a named person.

**Collateral / security** — An asset a borrower pledges to a lender. If the borrower
defaults, the lender can seize and sell it. A loan backed by collateral is "secured".

**Compound interest** — Interest calculated on the original principal plus all previously
accumulated interest. Over time it grows faster than simple interest.

**Credit (banking)** — (1) Money added to an account. (2) The ability to borrow, e.g. a
"line of credit".

**Credit score / credit report** — A number (e.g. CIBIL, Experian, FICO) summarising how
reliably a person has repaid past debt, based on a detailed credit report. Lenders use it to
decide whether to lend and at what rate.

**Creditor** — A person or institution to whom money is owed.

**Current account** — A bank account designed for frequent transactions (often for
businesses). It usually pays little or no interest but allows high transaction volumes and
overdrafts.

## D

**Debit** — Money taken out of an account.

**Debtor** — A person or institution that owes money.

**Default** — Failure to meet the legal obligations of a loan, typically by missing
repayments.

**Deflation** — A general fall in prices across an economy (opposite of inflation).

**Depreciation** — A fall in the value of an asset over time due to wear, age, or market
conditions.

**Diversification** — Spreading investments across different assets so that poor performance
in one does not sink the whole portfolio. "Don't put all your eggs in one basket."

**Dividend** — A share of a company's profits paid to shareholders.

## E

**EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment)** — A fixed monthly payment a borrower makes to repay a
loan, covering both interest and principal, so the loan is fully repaid by the end of the
term.

**Equity** — (1) Ownership in a company, represented by shares. (2) The portion of an asset
you truly own, e.g. home value minus outstanding mortgage.

**Escrow** — Money or assets held by a neutral third party until agreed conditions are met.

## F

**Fixed deposit (FD) / term deposit / CD** — A deposit locked in for a set period at a
fixed interest rate, usually higher than a savings account. Early withdrawal typically
incurs a penalty.

**Floating / variable interest rate** — An interest rate that moves up or down over the life
of a loan as a benchmark rate changes (opposite of fixed rate).

**Foreclosure / prepayment** — Repaying a loan in full before the scheduled end date.
Lenders may charge a prepayment fee, though many jurisdictions restrict this on
floating-rate personal loans.

## G

**Grace period** — A window after a payment due date during which no penalty or interest is
charged. Credit cards often have an interest-free grace period on purchases if the full
balance is paid.

**Gross vs. net** — "Gross" is an amount before deductions (tax, fees); "net" is what
remains after them.

## H

**Hedge** — An action or instrument used to reduce the risk of adverse price movements.

**Hypothecation** — Pledging a movable asset (like a vehicle) as security for a loan while
keeping possession of it.

## I

**IFSC (Indian Financial System Code)** — An 11-character code identifying a specific bank
branch for NEFT, RTGS, and IMPS transfers in India.

**Inflation** — A general rise in prices over time, which reduces the purchasing power of
money.

**Interest** — The cost of borrowing money, or the reward for saving/lending it, usually
expressed as an annual percentage.

**Instalment** — One of a series of scheduled payments toward a debt.

## K

**KYC (Know Your Customer)** — The regulatory process by which a bank verifies a customer's
identity and address (using documents like a passport, Aadhaar, PAN, or utility bill) before
and during a banking relationship, to prevent fraud and money laundering.

## L

**Liability** — A financial obligation or debt owed to others.

**Lien** — A lender's legal right to hold or sell an asset until a debt is repaid.

**Liquidity** — How quickly and cheaply an asset can be converted to cash without losing
value. Cash is the most liquid asset; property is relatively illiquid.

**Loan-to-value (LTV) ratio** — The loan amount as a percentage of the value of the asset
being financed. A lower LTV means the borrower contributes more of their own money.

## M

**Maturity** — The date on which a deposit, bond, or loan term ends and the principal
becomes due.

**Minimum balance** — The lowest balance a bank requires an account to hold; falling below
it may trigger a fee.

**Money market** — The market for short-term, low-risk lending and borrowing (typically
under one year).

**Mortgage / home loan** — A long-term loan used to buy property, secured by that property.

**Mutual fund** — A pooled investment vehicle that collects money from many investors and
invests it in a diversified portfolio of stocks, bonds, or other assets, managed by a
professional fund manager.

## N

**NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, UPI** — Indian electronic payment systems. See the "Payments and
transfers" topic for details.

**Net worth** — Total assets minus total liabilities; a snapshot of overall financial
position.

**Nominee** — A person named by an account holder to receive the account's funds if the
holder dies. A nominee is a custodian, not necessarily the legal heir.

**Non-performing asset (NPA)** — A loan on which the borrower has not made scheduled
payments for a specified period (90 days in India), so the lender classifies it as
distressed.

## O

**Overdraft** — A facility that lets an account go below zero up to an agreed limit.
Interest is charged on the overdrawn amount.

## P

**PAN (Permanent Account Number)** — A 10-character alphanumeric ID issued by India's tax
department, required for many financial transactions.

**Portfolio** — The complete collection of investments held by a person or institution.

**Premium (insurance)** — The amount paid, usually periodically, to keep an insurance policy
active.

**Principal** — The original sum of money borrowed or invested, excluding interest.

**Provisioning** — Money a bank sets aside to cover potential losses on loans that may not
be repaid.

## R

**Recurring deposit (RD)** — A deposit where a fixed sum is paid in every month for a set
term at a fixed interest rate.

**Repo rate** — The rate at which a central bank lends short-term funds to commercial banks
against government securities. A key tool for controlling inflation and liquidity.

**Return** — The gain or loss on an investment over a period, usually expressed as a
percentage of the amount invested.

**Risk** — The chance that an investment's actual outcome will differ from what was
expected, including the possibility of losing money. Higher potential returns generally come
with higher risk.

## S

**Savings account** — A deposit account that pays modest interest and is meant for holding
money not needed for daily spending.

**Secured vs. unsecured loan** — A secured loan is backed by collateral (home loan, car
loan); an unsecured loan is not (personal loan, most credit cards) and usually carries a
higher interest rate.

**Simple interest** — Interest calculated only on the original principal, not on accumulated
interest.

**Solvency** — The ability to meet long-term financial obligations.

**Spread** — The difference between two rates or prices, e.g. between the interest a bank
pays on deposits and charges on loans.

## T

**Tenure / term** — The length of time over which a loan is repaid or a deposit is held.

**Time value of money** — The principle that a sum of money is worth more today than the
same sum in the future, because it can be invested to earn a return.

## U

**Underwriting** — The process by which a lender or insurer assesses risk and decides
whether to approve an application and on what terms.

**UPI (Unified Payments Interface)** — An Indian real-time payment system that links bank
accounts to mobile apps, allowing instant transfers using a VPA ("UPI ID"), phone number,
or QR code.

## V

**Virtual Payment Address (VPA / UPI ID)** — An identifier like `name@bank` used to send or
receive money over UPI without sharing account numbers.

**Volatility** — The degree to which the price of an asset moves up and down over time.
Higher volatility means larger swings.

## Y

**Yield** — The income return on an investment, such as interest or dividends, expressed as
a percentage of its price.
