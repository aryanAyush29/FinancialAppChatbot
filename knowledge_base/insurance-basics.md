# Insurance Basics

Educational overview of how insurance works and the main types individuals encounter. Not
advice on which policy to buy.

## The core idea

Insurance pools risk. Many people pay small, regular amounts (**premiums**) into a fund; the
insurer uses that fund to pay large, unpredictable losses suffered by the few. You trade a
small certain cost for protection against a large uncertain one.

## Key terms

- **Premium** — what you pay to keep the policy active (monthly, quarterly, or yearly).
- **Sum assured / sum insured / coverage** — the maximum the insurer will pay.
- **Policy term** — how long the cover lasts.
- **Deductible / excess** — the portion of a claim you pay yourself before the insurer pays.
- **Co-payment** — a percentage of each claim you share (common in some health policies,
  especially for seniors).
- **Exclusions** — situations the policy does not cover (read these carefully).
- **Waiting period** — time after purchase before certain claims are allowed (e.g. for
  pre-existing conditions in health insurance).
- **Rider / add-on** — optional extra cover bought with the base policy (e.g. critical
  illness, accidental death).
- **Nominee / beneficiary** — who receives the payout.
- **Claim** — a request for the insurer to pay for a covered loss.
- **Free-look period** — a short window (e.g. 15–30 days) after buying a life/health policy
  to cancel for a refund if you disagree with the terms.
- **Underwriting** — the insurer's risk assessment that sets your premium or declines cover.
- **Claim settlement ratio** — the share of claims an insurer paid in a period; one input
  when comparing insurers.

## Main types for individuals

### Term life insurance
- Pure protection: pays the sum assured to your nominee if you die during the term; pays
  nothing if you survive it (that's why it's cheap).
- Purpose: replace your income and clear debts so dependants are not left financially
  stranded.
- Generally the most cost-efficient way to get a large life cover.

### Other life insurance (endowment, ULIP, whole life, money-back)
- Bundle insurance with savings or investment. Premiums are much higher for the same cover,
  and returns are often modest after charges.
- ULIPs invest part of the premium in market-linked funds; endowment/money-back plans give a
  guaranteed-plus-bonus payout.
- Understand the charges, lock-in, and surrender value before buying; "insurance" and
  "investment" goals can also be met separately (term plan + independent investments).

### Health insurance (mediclaim)
- Pays hospitalisation and related medical costs, via **cashless** treatment at network
  hospitals or **reimbursement**.
- Watch for: room-rent limits, disease-wise sub-limits, co-pay, waiting periods for
  pre-existing conditions and specific ailments, and exclusions.
- A **family floater** covers the whole family under one shared sum insured.
- A **top-up / super top-up** adds high-level cover above a threshold at low cost.
- Employer cover is useful but ends when the job does, so many people also hold an
  individual policy.

### Motor insurance
- **Third-party** cover is legally mandatory in India — it pays for injury or damage you
  cause to others.
- **Comprehensive** adds "own damage" cover for your vehicle (accident, theft, fire,
  natural events).
- **IDV (Insured Declared Value)** is the vehicle's current market value and the basis for a
  total-loss claim.
- **No-Claim Bonus (NCB)** discounts your premium for each claim-free year.

### Home / property insurance
- Covers the structure and/or contents against fire, theft, and specified perils. Often
  cheap relative to the value protected.

### Personal accident and critical illness
- **Personal accident** pays a lump sum for death or disability from an accident.
- **Critical illness** pays a lump sum on diagnosis of a listed serious illness, regardless
  of actual medical bills.

### Government and social insurance (India examples)
- PMJJBY (life), PMSBY (accident), and Ayushman Bharat / PM-JAY (health for eligible
  families) provide low-cost or free basic cover.

## How claims generally work

1. **Notify** the insurer promptly (immediately for hospitalisation or accident).
2. **Submit documents** — claim form, policy details, bills, reports, FIR where relevant.
3. **Assessment** — the insurer (or a surveyor/TPA) verifies the loss against the policy.
4. **Settlement** — approved amount is paid, minus any deductible/co-pay; or the claim is
   rejected with reasons (usually an exclusion, non-disclosure, or lapsed policy).
- **Disclosure matters:** hiding a pre-existing condition or prior claim can void the policy.
- If treated unfairly, escalate to the insurer's grievance cell, then the **Insurance
  Ombudsman** (India).

## Choosing cover (educational framing, not advice)

- Insure against losses you **could not absorb** yourself (death of an earner, a major
  hospital bill, a car write-off). Don't insure small, affordable losses — the premium and
  paperwork aren't worth it.
- Match the **sum assured** to the actual exposure: for term life, a common rule of thumb is
  10–15× annual income plus outstanding loans; for health, consider local hospitalisation
  costs.
- Compare **exclusions, waiting periods, sub-limits, and claim ratio**, not just the
  premium.
- Buy early — premiums for life and health cover rise with age and health issues.
- Keep beneficiaries and contact details updated; tell your nominee the policy exists.
