# Loans and Credit

Educational overview of how borrowing works, the main loan types, and how interest and
repayment are calculated. Not a recommendation to take or avoid any loan.

## Core concepts

- **Principal** — the amount borrowed.
- **Interest** — the price of borrowing, quoted as an annual rate.
- **Tenure / term** — how long you have to repay.
- **EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment)** — a level monthly payment that repays principal plus
  interest over the tenure. Early EMIs are mostly interest; later EMIs are mostly principal.
  This split is shown in an **amortisation schedule**.
- **APR (Annual Percentage Rate)** — interest plus mandatory fees expressed as one yearly
  percentage, so different offers can be compared fairly.

### How an EMI is calculated

For a loan of principal P, monthly interest rate r (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n months:

```
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)
```

Example: ₹10,00,000 at 9% per year for 20 years (n = 240, r = 0.0075) gives an EMI of about
₹8,997, and total payments of roughly ₹21.6 lakh — so about ₹11.6 lakh of interest over the
life of the loan. Longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises total interest paid.

## Fixed vs. floating interest

- **Fixed rate:** the rate stays the same for the whole tenure (or a fixed sub-period).
  Predictable EMIs; you don't benefit if market rates fall.
- **Floating rate:** the rate is tied to a benchmark (in India, usually the RBI **repo
  rate** via the bank's Repo Linked Lending Rate) plus a margin. EMIs or tenure change when
  the benchmark moves. Regulators require lenders to let borrowers switch to fixed or reset
  tenure when rates rise sharply.

## Secured vs. unsecured

- **Secured loan:** backed by collateral the lender can seize on default — e.g. home loan
  (property), car loan (vehicle, via hypothecation), gold loan, loan against FD or
  securities. Lower interest because the lender's risk is lower.
- **Unsecured loan:** no collateral — e.g. personal loan, most credit-card debt, student
  loans in some countries. Higher interest; approval depends heavily on income and credit
  score.

## Common loan types

| Loan | Typical purpose | Usually secured? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home loan / mortgage | Buy or build property | Yes (the property) | Long tenure (up to 30 yrs); LTV capped (e.g. 75–90%); tax benefits in some countries |
| Loan against property (LAP) | Any purpose, against owned property | Yes | Lower rate than personal loan; risk of losing the property |
| Car / vehicle loan | Buy a vehicle | Yes (the vehicle) | Tenure 1–7 yrs; vehicle hypothecated to lender until repaid |
| Personal loan | Any purpose | No | Fast, flexible, high rate; watch processing fees and prepayment terms |
| Education loan | Tuition and living costs | Sometimes | Moratorium during study; co-borrower usually required |
| Gold loan | Short-term cash needs | Yes (gold jewellery) | Quick disbursal; risk of auction if unpaid |
| Credit-card revolving balance | Short-term purchases | No | Very high effective rate if not repaid in full; see cards topic |
| Business / working-capital loan | Run or expand a business | Varies | May be a term loan or a credit line |

## Fees and charges to check before signing

- **Processing fee** (often 0.5–2% of the loan)
- **Prepayment / foreclosure charge** — many jurisdictions ban this on floating-rate
  personal loans to individuals, but it can apply to fixed-rate or business loans
- **Late payment penalty** and penal charges
- **Conversion / switch fee** to change your rate
- **Documentation, legal, and valuation charges** (common on home loans)
- **Loan insurance** bundled with the loan — usually optional; you can decline

## The borrowing process

1. **Eligibility check** — lender looks at income, existing obligations, employment
   stability, and credit score.
2. **Application + documents** — ID, address, income proof (salary slips, bank statements,
   tax returns), and property/vehicle papers for secured loans.
3. **Underwriting / appraisal** — lender assesses risk and, for secured loans, values the
   collateral.
4. **Sanction letter** — states the approved amount, rate, tenure, EMI, and conditions.
5. **Disbursal** — funds released, often directly to a seller or institution.
6. **Repayment** — EMIs by auto-debit; a **no-dues / NOC** certificate and release of
   collateral once fully repaid.

## Key ratios lenders use

- **Fixed Obligations to Income Ratio (FOIR) / Debt-to-Income (DTI):** total monthly debt
  payments ÷ monthly income. Lenders often want this below roughly 40–50%.
- **Loan-to-Value (LTV):** loan ÷ asset value. Lower LTV = more borrower "skin in the game".
- **Credit score:** see the cards-and-credit-score topic.

## What "default" means and why it matters

Missing payments leads to late fees, a lower credit score, and — after a defined period
(90 days in India) — classification of the loan as a **non-performing asset**. For secured
loans the lender can eventually repossess and sell the collateral (for example, under the
SARFAESI Act in India for certain loans). Talking to the lender early about restructuring or
a temporary EMI reduction is usually better than silent non-payment.

## Ideas for managing borrowing (educational, not advice)

- Borrow for assets that hold or grow value or raise earning power; be cautious about debt
  for consumption that quickly loses value.
- Compare APR, not just the headline interest rate.
- A shorter tenure costs more per month but far less in total interest.
- Keep total EMIs a modest share of income so a shock (job loss, illness) is survivable.
- Read the sanction letter's fine print on rate resets, penal charges, and prepayment.
