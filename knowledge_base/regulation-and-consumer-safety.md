# Regulation, Consumer Rights, and Fraud Safety

Educational overview of who regulates finance (with an India focus), your rights as a
customer, and how to recognise and respond to fraud.

## Who regulates what (India)

| Regulator | Oversees |
|---|---|
| **RBI** (Reserve Bank of India) | Banks, NBFCs, payment systems, monetary policy, foreign exchange |
| **SEBI** (Securities and Exchange Board of India) | Stock exchanges, mutual funds, brokers, investment advisers, listed companies |
| **IRDAI** | Insurance companies and intermediaries |
| **PFRDA** | Pension funds and the National Pension System |
| **NPCI** | Retail payment infrastructure (UPI, IMPS, RuPay, NACH) — an umbrella body under RBI |
| **Ministry of Corporate Affairs / IBBI** | Company law, insolvency and bankruptcy |

Other countries have equivalents — e.g. the U.S. has the Federal Reserve, FDIC, OCC, SEC,
and CFPB; the U.K. has the FCA and PRA.

## Deposit and investor protection

- **Bank deposits (India):** insured by DICGC up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank.
- **Bank deposits (U.S.):** insured by FDIC up to $250,000 per depositor per bank per
  ownership category.
- **Investments are not principal-protected.** Regulators protect against fraud and
  misconduct, not against market losses. Brokerage safeguards (segregated client funds,
  investor protection funds) cover broker default, not falling prices.

## Your rights as a bank customer

Regulators expect banks to follow a **fair practices / customer rights** framework,
typically including:

- **Right to fair treatment** — no discrimination; clear, non-misleading communication.
- **Right to transparency** — full disclosure of interest, fees, and terms before you sign,
  in a language you understand; a one-page **Key Facts Statement** for retail loans in
  India.
- **Right to suitability** — products offered should suit your needs and profile;
  no mis-selling of bundled insurance or investments.
- **Right to privacy** — your information is confidential and shared only with consent or
  legal requirement.
- **Right to grievance redress** — a defined complaints process and compensation where due.
- **No misuse of recovery practices** — debt collection must follow rules on timing,
  conduct, and dignity.

## Complaint escalation (India)

1. **Bank / company grievance channel** first — branch, customer care, nodal officer. Note
   the complaint reference number.
2. If unresolved in **30 days** or you're unsatisfied, escalate to the relevant ombudsman
   under the **RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme** (banks, NBFCs, payment system
   participants), the **Insurance Ombudsman**, or **SEBI SCORES** for securities matters.
3. Ombudsman services are free. Beyond that, consumer courts or other legal remedies remain
   available.

## Recognising financial fraud

**Common scams:**
- **Phishing / smishing / vishing** — fake emails, SMS, or calls impersonating your bank,
  asking you to "verify" details, click a link, or share an OTP.
- **KYC-update scam** — "your account will be blocked today; update KYC via this link/app."
- **Remote-access scam** — you're told to install AnyDesk/TeamViewer so a "technician" can
  fix an issue, then they drain the account.
- **Refund / overpayment scam** — "we sent you money by mistake, approve this request to
  reverse it" (approving actually sends money out via UPI).
- **Job, loan, lottery, and investment scams** — upfront "processing fee" for a promised
  benefit; guaranteed high returns.
- **QR-code scam** — scanning a QR **pays** money; it never receives it. Fraudsters ask
  sellers to scan a QR to "get paid".
- **SIM swap** — attacker ports your number to intercept OTPs.
- **Fake customer-care numbers** from search engines or social media.
- **Digital arrest** — impostors posing as police/agencies keep you on a video call and
  coerce transfers.

**Red flags:** urgency and threats, requests for OTP/PIN/CVV/password, links to non-official
domains, pressure to install apps or scan codes, "guaranteed" returns, requests to move
money to a "safe account", and payment demands in gift cards or crypto.

## Safe habits

- Banks and regulators **never** ask for your OTP, PIN, CVV, net-banking password, or card
  number by phone, SMS, email, or chat.
- You do **not** enter a PIN or approve a "collect request" to **receive** money.
- Type bank URLs yourself or use the official app; check for the padlock and correct domain.
- Keep your phone's OS and apps updated; use a screen lock and app lock; enable transaction
  alerts.
- Use strong, unique passwords and enable two-factor authentication; never reuse the
  net-banking password elsewhere.
- Verify customer-care numbers only from the bank's official website or the back of your
  card.
- Review statements regularly; set daily transaction and channel limits.
- Don't share account statements, Aadhaar/PAN images, or card photos over chat.

## If you're defrauded — act fast

1. **Call the bank immediately** and freeze the card/account; use the official 24x7 fraud
   line.
2. In India, report on the **National Cybercrime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in)** or
   call **1930** as soon as possible — quick reporting improves the chance of blocking or
   recovering funds.
3. File a written complaint with the bank; keep the acknowledgement.
4. **Zero / limited liability:** under RBI rules, if you report an unauthorised electronic
   transaction promptly and the fault is not yours, your liability can be zero or capped.
   Delay increases the amount you may have to bear.
5. Change passwords and PINs; check for unauthorised payees, rules, or SIM changes.
6. Escalate to the Ombudsman if the bank's resolution is unsatisfactory.
